CFAACREDIT/FINANCIAL ANALYSIS AUTOMATION · USER GUIDE

User guide: from a ticker or a set of statements to a full credit analysis

For credit analysts, lenders, investment professionals and students. No coding needed once the app is running.

What the app does, in one paragraph. You give it a public company's ticker or a private company's financial statements. It builds the financial spread, rates the company on a credit scorecard (Reported and Lease-adjusted bases), adds an indicative S&P-style rating, benchmarks the company against its sector and named public competitors, runs stress tests, checks the data for errors, and lists every judgment call it had to make. An optional team of AI analysts then writes the narrative: credit view, SWOT, economic and sector analysis, and accounting findings. Code calculates every number; the AI only reads and writes, and every number it cites is checked against the data.

1. Starting the app

Follow the README in the project folder (about 5 minutes the first time):

python -m venv .venv
.venv\Scripts\activate          (macOS/Linux: source .venv/bin/activate)
pip install -r requirements.txt
python app.py

Open http://127.0.0.1:8050. For the AI-written sections, put PORTKEY_API_KEY (or OPENAI_API_KEY) in a .env file in the project folder or one folder above it. Without a key, everything except the AI-written text and the chat still works.

2. Quick start: four workflows

1One public company
Keep Public company and Single ticker. Type LUV, press GO. Results appear in 10–30 seconds (about a minute the first time a company runs with AI).
2Compare competitors
Click Compare companies, type LUV and ULCC, add more with + Add company (up to 10), press COMPARE.
3A private company
Click Private company, upload the statements (Excel or PDF), check the lines the app read, pick the sector, press Confirm & analyze.
4Review and share
Read tab 1, go deeper in tabs 2–11, ask questions in Ask the analyst, then EXPORT to Excel or a Word/PDF memo.
Tip: untick AI analyst team to explore for free. Ratings, data, checks, stress tests and exports all still work; only the written analysis is skipped.

5. How to read the results

Credit score (0–100)

Higher is stronger. It comes from the Reported-basis scorecard. The coverage label tells you how much of the scorecard had data: Full opinion (90%+), Substantial (67–90%), Limited (provisional) (below 67%). A rating is always given; with limited data, treat it as provisional.

The three ratings

RatingMeaning
ReportedThe scorecard on the numbers as reported. Always shown first.
Lease-adjustedSame scorecard with lease liabilities added to debt and part of lease cost treated as interest.
S&P-style (indicative)Business risk × financial risk anchor, then modifiers (liquidity cap, sponsor ownership). Some weighting benchmarks follow S&P's published method. Not an official S&P rating.

Agency ratings (S&P, Moody's, Fitch) appear only when the company discloses them in its filings; you can also enter them yourself in tab 3.

Ratio messages

Ratio is Infinite: the denominator is zero (e.g., no debt). Data is Missing: an input isn't in the data.

Red flags

RED means the numbers don't hold together: the balance sheet doesn't balance, subtotals don't add up, debt looks incomplete, or an implied interest rate is implausible. The app never "fixes" the numbers: the finances are the finances. It flags them so you can check the source or correct a figure in tab 5.

6. Private companies in detail

  1. Upload an Excel workbook or a text PDF (not a scanned image), up to 10 MB and 80 pages. Include the notes: the app reads lease, debt, covenant, related-party and going-concern notes and the accountant's report.
  2. Check what the app read. It shows each statement line, the field it matched, the periods, the units it detected (thousands or millions) and the statement quality (audited, reviewed, compiled, management). Fix any line with the dropdown. Ask the Accounting Analyst to map … maps lines it couldn't match.
  3. Pick the sector and press Confirm & analyze.
  4. Tab 7 compares the company with named public competitors from SEC filings and shows which ones are used.

Try it with data/samples/sample_private_reviewed_statements.pdf. It contains a deliberate balance-sheet error so you can see the red flag.

7. Comparing companies

Comparison mode shows a side-by-side table with the best value in each row highlighted, a credit-score chart, a ranking with reasons, and financial, SWOT and economic write-ups. Use the VIEW toggle (COMPARISON | LUV | ULCC …) to open any one company in full. To save tokens, comparisons reuse saved AI work and make one AI call for the write-up. If a company hasn't had its AI analysis yet, open it and press Run AI analyst team.

9. Ask the analyst (chat)

Ask anything about the analysis on screen, for example: "Why is the lease-adjusted rating lower?", "How much debt matures in the next two years?", "What drives the weak EBITDA margin?". Answers use only this analysis's data and point to the tab with the detail. If the data doesn't contain the answer, the chat says so rather than guessing.

  • Starter buttons such as "Why did it get this rating?", "What would move the rating up or down?" and "How does it compare with its peers?" are answered by code at no AI cost.
  • Other questions cost about 900–1,000 tokens. Asking the same question again is free.
  • Switching company starts a new conversation. Limit: 30 questions per hour.

10. Exports and the watchlist

  • Excel: summary, scorecard, statements, periods, peers, debt structure, stress tests, red flags, assumptions and your analyst case, one sheet each. In comparison view it exports the comparison.
  • Memo (Word / PDF): a nine-section credit memo ready for an investment-committee pack.
  • + Watchlist: saves a public company in this browser. Check for new filings looks for a newer 10-K or 10-Q and re-scores only companies that have one, showing any rating change. It runs when you press the button; it does not send alerts while the app is closed.

12. Limits and troubleshooting

SituationWhat to do
"Ticker not found"Use the US exchange ticker (e.g., BRK-B). Only SEC filers are covered.
Coverage shows "Limited"Some inputs aren't in the company's XBRL tags; check tab 10 for hints and fill blanks in tab 5.
"Debt looks incomplete" red flagThe company reports debt in its own tags; enter total debt in tab 5.
Scanned PDF can't be readUpload a text PDF or the Excel version.
AI sections say "not run"Tick AI analyst team and press GO again, or check that a key is in .env.
"Too many requests"Rate limits protect cost; wait the minutes shown.
Use as analysis support. Implied ratings are not official S&P ratings, and the app is not investment advice. Some of the weighting benchmarks in the analysis are drawn from S&P's rating methodology. Always review the red flags and open assumptions before relying on a result.
