Credit/Financial Analysis Automation

Understand a company in depth in minutes instead of hours.

Credit/Financial Analysis Automation gives credit and investment analysts a complete picture of a company in one place:

  • Financial position: the income statement, balance sheet and cash flows, spread and checked for errors;
  • Credit risk: a credit score and ratings, the ratios behind them, and stress tests;
  • Debt and credit structure: what the company owes, to whom, and when it comes due, plus its liquidity;
  • Economic context: the economy (macro) and the company's industry and competitors (micro).

What used to take an analyst up to three hours of gathering and calculating is ready in minutes, so the analyst's time goes to judgment. Analysts get clear, complete information for institutional lending, debt investment and credit decisions, including for private companies that have no public credit rating. Code calculates every number; AI writes the analysis, and every figure it cites is checked against the data.

2. Motivation

Who has the problem

Credit analysts, leveraged-finance and private-credit teams, commercial and middle-market lenders, and investment professionals who must form a view on a company's ability to repay debt. The same work also sits on the desk of every analyst who covers a sector, and of students learning credit.

How they handle it today

A thorough first look at one company means:

  • pulling the latest 10-K and 10-Q and keying the income statement, balance sheet and cash flows into a spreading template;
  • building last-twelve-months figures from year-to-date numbers;
  • adjusting debt for leases and pensions, computing a dozen ratios and mapping them to a rating scale;
  • finding comparable companies and their ratios;
  • reading the debt, lease and commitments notes for maturities and off-balance-sheet items;
  • checking the macro backdrop, and only then writing it up.

For a private borrower it's harder still: the statements arrive as PDFs or spreadsheets in the company's own format, often unaudited, and there are no public comparables on hand.

What it costs

In my own work this takes up to 3 hours per company before any real thinking happens. Most of that time is gathering and calculating, not judging. It is also where errors creep in: a mis-keyed balance-sheet line, a missed lease liability or an annualization mistake can move a rating by several notches. The professional alternatives are expensive: a Bloomberg Terminal or S&P Capital IQ seat is widely reported at tens of thousands of dollars per user per year, and even those leave the spreading, peer selection and write-up to the analyst.

Honest differentiation. CFAA is not a replacement for a terminal: it has no market prices, spreads or bond-level recovery analysis. It is an add-on that does the first 90% of a credit review (the spread, the scorecard, the peer set, the economic context and a first draft) so the analyst starts from a complete, checkable picture. Three design choices set it apart from a general AI assistant:

  • The AI never does math. Ratings come from my credit spread scorecard, ported from Excel and regression-tested against the original workbooks. The AI cannot change a rating, only explain it.
  • Every number and quote is verified. An AI output that cites a figure not in its data, or a quote not in the filing, is rejected and retried, and is escalated after three attempts.
  • Nothing is silently assumed. Judgment calls (business risk, lease treatment, cash netting, missing revolver data) are listed with options, and changing one re-scores instantly without AI cost.

How well it works

  • Faithful to the original model: the Python scorecard reproduces the Excel credit spread workbooks (regression tests). SEC-derived spreads matched the hand-built workbooks on 33 of 48 values exactly, with every difference explained by source (for example, a different debt tag).
  • Private path proven: Southwest's public numbers entered through the private-company upload path matched the public path on 21 of 21 periods. A deliberately mis-keyed equity figure in the sample PDF is flagged in red.
  • Calibration: indicative ratings line up with agency ratings disclosed in filings. For example, Coca-Cola scores A+/A against S&P A+ and Moody's A1; Delta scores BBB/BBB- against Moody's Baa2. Known gaps (Pfizer's one-off items, Caterpillar's captive finance arm) are documented.
  • Coverage: 10 of 11 large public companies tested reach a full scorecard. Ford, whose debt sits in company-specific tags, is flagged "Debt looks incomplete" instead of being given a false rating.

4. The agents

The app is a small credit team: four specialist analysts, a manager and a writer, plus three on-demand agents. Code decides what each agent sees: a compact brief of computed figures, or screened filing text. Agents have no tools and no web access, so they can only read what they're given and write a structured answer.

Model and why

gpt-6-luna through Portkey with the course key, set in one place (OPENAI_MODEL overrides it). Outputs use JSON-schema structured output (PydanticAI NativeOutput), because the course model rejects function tools, and because schemas make every answer machine-checkable. Reasoning effort is set to low: the agents interpret facts that code has already computed, so deep reasoning adds hidden tokens without improving the result.
